The Stars

Those pieces are the stars.

Every project scatters knowledge across tools, conversations, and people. Each fragment is a star — small on its own, powerful when connected.

Specs & Requirements

Requirements that define what needs to be built — often scattered across documents and versions.

Slack Conversations

Decisions that happen in real-time but get buried under the velocity of daily communication.

Email Threads

Concerns, approvals, and context that live in long threads only a few people ever read.

GitHub & Repos

Naming conventions, patterns, and technical decisions embedded inside files and commit history.

Meeting Notes

Dependencies, blockers, and context shared verbally — then roughly captured in notes nobody revisits.

Jira & Tickets

Stakeholder assumptions and requirements hiding in comment threads inside issue trackers.

The Pattern

That pattern is
the Constellation.

Constellation gathers the stars of your project universe and reveals the shape they make together. Not by replacing the way teams work, but by grounding their work in what already exists.

What looked disconnected
becomes connected.
What felt vague

What was buried
becomes visible.
What was assumed

The Hidden Tax

The cost of confusion is rarely dramatic at first.

It shows up in small delays, repeated conversations, conflicting implementation, architectural drift, and the quiet frustration of "I thought we already decided that."

Constellation exists to remove that hidden tax — by creating clarity from the material teams already have.

Most teams are not failing because they lack intelligence.
They are struggling because their knowledge is fragmented.
